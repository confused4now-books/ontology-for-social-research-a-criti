# Glossary

The glossary is built by the authoring app from the terms your chapters use, and
then edited by hand. Until you run it, this file is empty apart from this note.

This file has to exist, and it has to be at the top of the repository. The
authoring app decides that a folder is a textbook by finding `chapters/`,
`assets/` and `glossary.md` together (`authoring-assistant/app/convert.py`,
`_is_vault_root`). Without all three it refuses to place a converted chapter's
pictures rather than guess.

## Critical Realism

However, as we come from the theoretical background of Critical Realism, we cannot fully avoid presenting these views, and indeed could not present this theory of interdisciplinarity without some assumptions. (First used in [[chapters/introduction|Introduction]].)

## individuals and groups

The individuals and groups debate asks where causal power is located: how much explanatory work is done by individuals and their interactions, and how much by collectivities, institutions and structures whose emergent properties and causal powers are not reducible to their members. (First used in [[chapters/introduction|Introduction]].)

## intransitive

Reality as it exists and operates independently of our knowledge of it, the structures, mechanisms, relations, and entities that have causal powers, whether or not we have theorized them adequately.

## material and ideational

Third, the material and ideational debate asks how material interests, economic relations and physical resources stand in relation to meanings, norms, beliefs and discursive structures. (First used in [[chapters/introduction|Introduction]].)

## Ontology

The study of what exists, how different aspects of reality are constituted, and what properties and causal powers they possess.

## structure and agency

The second, and at a higher level of abstraction, the structure and agency debate asks how the patterns that precede any particular action relate to the actions through which people reproduce, transform or resist the social conditions around them, and therefore what is treated as durable, what as open, and by what…. (First used in [[chapters/introduction|Introduction]].)

## transcendence and immanence

And finally, the highest level of abstraction, the transcendence and immanence debate asks a twofold question of universality and causality; once in regards to if and how there are universal features of society, or if each society is entirely specific to its space-time constellation; the second asks about conceptions…. (First used in [[chapters/introduction|Introduction]].)

## transcendental

The conditions of possibility for something to be there. Generally considered to originate with Kant, transcendental arguments aim to show that X is a necessary condition for Y to happen, and as we can agree to Y, we can ask what would be needed for this to be possible in the first place.

## transitive

Our mind-dependent knowledge of mind-independent reality, including out historically developing body of theories, concepts, models, and explanatory frameworks through which we attempt to understand the mind-independent reality (the intransitive).
